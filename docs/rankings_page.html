Matt Mahoney maintains the two largest public compression benchmarks: the Large Text Compression Benchmark and the Silesia Open Source Compression Benchmark. This page merges them into one ranking of the programs that appear in both, so a compressor has to do well on Wikipedia text and on mixed data at the same time.

FluidCompressor is open source: its code, the sealed test files and a Windows build are on GitHub under AGPL-3.0. On a Windows laptop with a Core i3, every sealed-test archive came out byte-identical to the Mac's.

The two benchmarks

Merged ranking

Score is the geometric mean of a program's two sizes, each divided by the best size in the merged set, so both benchmarks weigh equally. Lower is better; 1.0 would mean the best result on both. Speeds are the compress and decompress times per byte listed by the Large Text Compression Benchmark.

How the merge works
  • A program is its first name token without a separated version number. Digits that are part of a name are kept, so paq8l, paq9a, fp8 and bzip2 stay distinct. A pipeline counts as its own program, except a preprocessor packaged by the same author, the way the Large Text Compression Benchmark lists drt|lpaq9m as lpaq9m.
  • Each program is represented by its best entry in each table: on enwik9 by that benchmark's own measure, compressed enwik9 plus the zipped decompressor; on Silesia by the total of its 12 files.
  • Only programs listed in both tables are ranked, plus FluidCompressor.
  • The score divides each size by the best in the merged set and takes the geometric mean of the two. Ordering by total bytes, or by the mean of the two ranks, gives almost the same list.
